Small jobs

The single most mentioned thing in the reviews, by a distance. Counted five separate times, more than any other topic. This is the list that never quite justifies calling a contractor: a handle, a fixture, a patch, a door that will not latch, a shelf that needs to hold something heavier than it was built for.

Small jobs are also where most handymen quietly stop returning calls, because they do not pay like a renovation. Ours get scheduled and finished.

Named in five reviews

Doors and sliding-door replacement

Sliders swapped for French pairs, doors rehung, frames squared, thresholds and weatherseals renewed. When the old unit comes out the opening itself usually needs attention: packing, flashing, a sill that has taken years of water. That is part of the job rather than a change order.

See Case 01 for one of these end to end.

Named in two reviews, including Sonia A.

Lighting installation

Fixtures inside and out, plus the switching to go with them. Frequently fitted in the same visit as the larger job. One customer got a new French door and new lights on the same day, which is the advantage of one person carrying more than one trade.

Named in two reviews, including Sonia A.

Irrigation repair and maintenance

Heads, valves, timers, broken laterals, and the slow leaks you only catch by watching the meter with everything off. A Central Florida irrigation system runs most of the year, so it wears out like something that runs most of the year, and a stuck zone can waste more water in a month than the repair costs.

Named in two reviews

Storm damage and water intrusion

Wind-driven water finding a path through exterior joints is the classic Florida failure, and the hardest to diagnose, because it only reveals itself in weather. That is exactly why it survives inspection after inspection. We chase the path rather than the stain.

One customer had this in a two-year-old house, every year, for two years. See Case 02.

Reconfiguration and trade coordination

Jobs that need another vendor on site, or a layout altered rather than replaced. One customer's job needed both and called the reconfiguration element tricky, meaning it was not a swap, it was a rethink. Those are the reason the phone gets answered at odd hours.
